## 0. The plan in one page

**Goal:** when someone in Brits and the surrounding Madibeng area searches Google, asks Google's AI, or asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Copilot for hardware, plumbing, electrical, tools, paint or solar, **Empire is the answer they get, and they can see exactly what the store can get for them.**

**The core insight: three different games, won in three different places.**

| # | Query type | Examples | Where it is won | What wins it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Where" (local)** | hardware store Brits · solar shop near me · plumbing supplies Harties | Google Map Pack, Google Maps "Ask", Gemini, ChatGPT local cards | Google Business Profile, reviews, consistent listings, proximity |
| 2 | **"What / which / how" (advice)** | what size inverter do I need · geyser element hard water or soft · best roof waterproofing | AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity, classic results | Pages on our site that answer the question better than anyone, written by people who know the trade |
| 3 | **"This exact thing" (product)** | Sunsynk 8kW Brits · Geo 15mm ball valve · RCSB18540 · Ingco angle grinder | Classic Google results, Bing, AI shopping answers | Clean, fast product and category pages with codes, specs and the local store attached |

Most local hardware sites only play game 1. National online shops (Solar Shop, Builders, Takealot) win game 3 on price. **Empire's opening is to own game 1 in Madibeng, win game 2 with real counter knowledge, and win game 3 wherever a local or code search is involved.**

**The 12 actions that matter most (in order):**

1. Claim and fully build the **Google Business Profile** before launch (section 5). It is the biggest single local ranking factor and the only source Gemini and Google Maps "Ask" trust fully.
2. Start a **review engine** from day one: ask every customer who gets a reply to an enquiry. AI assistants filter out businesses below about 4.0 to 4.3 stars (section 5.4).
3. Lock one **NAP** (name, address, phone) and use it character for character everywhere (section 5.1).
4. List the store on **Bing Places**, **Apple Business Connect**, **Foursquare** and the core SA directories. ChatGPT and Copilot read Bing and third-party listings, not Google (section 6).
5. Get listed on every **supplier's "where to buy" / dealer locator** (Sunsynk, Deye, Geo, Ingco, Promac, Duram, Flash Harry and the rest). These are high-trust links and the exact pages AI tools read when someone asks "where can I buy Duram in Brits" (section 6.3).
6. Build the catalogue so it is **crawlable in plain HTML**, with **one page per product family, not one per code**. About 6,300 rows become roughly 2,800 useful pages (section 3).
7. Control **filters, search and pagination** so Google never sees millions of junk URLs (section 3.4).
8. Apply the **keyword map** to every department, category, brand and priority product page (section 4 and the workbook).
9. Publish a **"Counter advice" hub** with about 24 guides in the first 6 months, each answering a real question customers ask at the counter, following the seasonal calendar (section 7).
10. Add a **Store facts / FAQ** page that states every fact an AI might need: what we sell, brands, hours, order-in, delivery, payment, languages, directions (section 7.3).
11. Set up **Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools (with IndexNow), GBP insights and analytics** before launch, and track a fixed set of **AI prompts** every month (section 9).
12. Run everything on **real content only**. No AI-written filler, no copy-paste town pages, no fake reviews. Google's August 2026 spam update removed whole sites for exactly that (section 3.5).

**Two decisions the client needs to make** (details in section 11):
- **Show prices on some products?** Even "from" prices on solar kits and top sellers would unlock Google Shopping free listings and product rich results. Today's "no prices" brief rules those out.
- **Do they install solar, or refer to an installer?** "Solar installation Brits" is one of the biggest local keyword groups. We need to know which side of it Empire sits on.

## 3. Technical build and information architecture

This section changes or adds to the website plan. Items marked **(change)** alter something already in `Empire-Website-Plan.md`.

### 3.1 Domain and URLs

- **Domain:** use a **.co.za** domain (for example `empiresolar.co.za` or `empirehardware.co.za`). The ccTLD tells Google the site is South African. Client item 14 in the website plan.
- **URL rules:** lowercase, hyphens, no dates, no IDs, no supplier names in category paths.

| Page type | URL pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Home | `/` | |
| Catalogue home | `/catalogue` | |
| Department | `/catalogue/{department}` | `/catalogue/solar-backup-power` |
| Category | `/catalogue/{department}/{category}` | `/catalogue/plumbing/geysers` |
| Sub-category | `/catalogue/{department}/{category}/{sub}` | `/catalogue/plumbing/valves/ball-valves` |
| Product family | `/p/{brand}-{product}-{key spec}` | `/p/geo-geyser-pressure-control-valve-400kpa` |
| Brand (new) | `/brands/{brand}` | `/brands/sunsynk` |

| Store FAQ (new) | `/faq` | |

### 3.2 One page per product family, not per code (change)

The workbook has 6,319 rows, but many are the same product in different sizes or packs:

- **Geo:** 730 rows are `_PP` packed versions of a bulk item (same product, branded bag). Names follow "Family – Size" (for example "Float Valve – 15mm", "Float Valve – 22mm").
- **AirCraft:** 1,056 rows, mostly push-in and hose fittings that differ only by size.
- **WACO:** ABB ranges listed once per rating (for example MCBs, contactors).
- **Ingco:** the same name ("Angle Grinder", "Cordless Drill") appears for different models.

**Rule:** one indexable page per **product family**, with a **variants table** listing every size, pack and code (bulk and `_PP` side by side). Each variant can have its own "Add to list" row. A rough grouping by name gives about **2,800 family pages** from 6,319 rows:

| Supplier | Rows | Approx. family pages |
|---|---|---|
| Geo Plumbing | 1,850 | ~410 |
| WACO | 1,502 | ~590 |
| AirCraft | 1,056 | ~310 |
| Ruwag (incl. Harden, tesa) | 903 | ~710 |
| Ingco | 510 | ~255 (after splitting by model) |
| Others (solar, paint, Wadfow) | 498 | ~495 |

This is an estimate from name patterns; the mapping script in build phase 1 sets the final families. **Why it matters:** fewer, richer pages rank better, look nothing like "scaled content", and match how people search ("float valve" not "BFV22_PP").

**Code search still works:** every code stays on the family page (visible text, not hidden), and site search jumps straight to the right variant. A URL like `/p/geo-float-valve?code=BFV22` can pre-select the size, with a canonical tag to the family page.

**Ingco and WACO names need enriching.** "Angle Grinder AG24008" tells Google nothing. Add the key spec to the name during data mapping: "Ingco 2400W 230mm Angle Grinder (AG24008)". Take specs from Ingco's own site. This is a real data task: budget for it in build phase 1.

### 3.3 Indexation tiers

| Tier | Pages | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Core** (~130 pages) | Home, Catalogue, 9 departments, ~70 categories, ~40 key sub-categories, brand pages, About, Contact, FAQ, advice guides | Indexed. Hand-written intro and FAQs. Internal links from navigation. In sitemap |
| **B. Product families with substance** (~2,000) | Families with a photo **or** a spec/variants table of real data | Indexed. Template content from real data only |
| **C. Thin families** (the rest) | No photo, no details, a one- or two-word name (for example "SINGLE PHASE", "BOWL – 160", "Brace Flat") | **Live and searchable on site, `noindex, follow`** until they get a photo or specs. Promote to B as data improves |
| **D. Utility** | `/search`, `/enquiry`, filtered URLs, sort orders | `noindex` or blocked (3.4) |

Data check behind this: **1,398 rows have no image** (all 1,056 AirCraft rows, plus Promac 96, Fivestar 84, Duram 68, Flash Harry 37, Africa Paints 29, Hanchu 20) and **4,036 rows have no details text**. Paint and waterproofing products have no photos but are high-value searches, so they are a priority for photos (a tin on the shelf, shot in store, is fine).

### 3.4 Filters, sort, pagination and search (change)

The website plan saves filters in the URL (good for sharing on WhatsApp) and uses a "Show 36 more" button. Both need SEO guard rails:

1. **Pagination must be crawlable.** Keep the "Show 36 more" button, but it must be a real link (`<a href="?page=2">`) that works without JavaScript, and each page must render its products in HTML. A button that only loads more through JavaScript hides products from crawlers, and most AI crawlers do not run JavaScript at all.
2. **Filter URLs** (`?brand=geo&size=15mm`) get `<link rel="canonical">` pointing to the unfiltered category, and are disallowed in robots.txt for multi-filter combinations.
3. **Filters that match real searches become real pages** with clean URLs and their own title, H1 and intro. Examples: `/catalogue/plumbing/valves/ball-valves`, `/catalogue/power-tools/grinders/cordless` ("cordless angle grinder"), `/catalogue/plumbing/pipe-fittings/hdpe-fittings`. The keyword map lists these.
4. **Sort orders** (`?sort=`) are never indexed.
5. **Site search results** (`/search?q=`) are `noindex` and never linked in a crawlable way.
6. **Watch the site search log.** What people type into Empire's search box is free keyword research. Review the top 50 terms and the "no results" terms monthly. Add synonyms, and create a category page when a term keeps appearing.

### 3.5 Staying clear of scaled-content problems

Google's August 2026 update punished sites that turned data feeds into thousands of thin pages. Empire's catalogue is legitimate (a real store, real stock, a real order-in service), but the pages must look and act like it.

**Do:**
- Build product pages from **real data**: name, brand, codes, sizes, pack quantities, specs, photos, category, related items, and the store's order-in promise.
- Write intros and FAQs **by hand** for the ~130 Tier A pages, using counter knowledge. Short and true beats long and generic.
- Add **"Counter tip"** notes (one or two sentences from staff) to the top 100 products over time. For example: "Most Brits houses use a 400kPa geyser. Check the rating plate on your geyser before buying."
- Use **the store's own photos** where possible. Supplier photos are shared by every stockist; your own are unique.

**Do not:**
- Generate product descriptions with AI at scale.
- Paste supplier marketing copy as the main text.
- Create pages for each town ("Hardware store Hartbeespoort", "Hardware store Mooinooi") that swap the town name into the same text. Those are doorway pages. Section 4.1 shows the right way to cover nearby towns.

### 3.6 Structured data (JSON-LD)

Google says no special schema is needed for AI features, but structured data still helps every engine understand exactly who and what the store is, and it powers rich results.

| Page | Schema | Key fields |
|---|---|---|
| Every page (in the layout) | `HardwareStore` (a type of `Store`) with a fixed `@id` (for example `https://domain/#store`) | `name`, `logo`, `image`, `address`, `geo`, `telephone`, `url`, `openingHoursSpecification` (plus `specialOpeningHoursSpecification` for public holidays), `areaServed` (list of places from 1.3), `sameAs` (GBP link, Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, YouTube, Bing Places, Hellopeter), `brand` list of suppliers, `knowsLanguage`, `paymentAccepted`, `currenciesAccepted: ZAR` |
| Home | `WebSite` + the store above | `name`, `url` |
| Category, sub-category, product | `BreadcrumbList` | |
| Product family | `Product` (or `ProductGroup` with `hasVariant` for sizes) | `name`, `brand`, `sku`, `mpn`, `image`, `category`, `additionalProperty` for sizes and ratings. **No `offers` unless a price is shown** (see note) |
| FAQ and advice pages | `FAQPage` where there is a real Q&A list, `Article` for guides with `author` (a named staff member) and `dateModified` | FAQ rich results are now limited by Google, but other engines still read it |
| About | `AboutPage`, and `Person` for named staff if they agree | |

**Note on Product without prices:** this markup is valid and useful for understanding, but it will **not** earn product rich results, and Search Console may list the pages as having "missing offers". That is expected and does not harm the site. Never add fake prices or ratings to fill the gap: that breaks Google's rules. See decision 11.1.

Validate with Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator in build phase 8.

### 3.7 Crawling, sitemaps and fast indexing

- **robots.txt:** allow Googlebot, Bingbot, **OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, Perplexity-User, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User** and Applebot. Disallow `/search`, `/enquiry` and multi-filter parameters.
- **Training crawlers** (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, CCBot): a local store benefits from AI models "knowing" it, and has little content worth protecting. **Recommendation: allow.** The only caution is supplier photos, which Empire does not own; that is a supplier-permission question, not a crawler one.
- **Cloudflare check:** the site uses Cloudflare Turnstile. If the domain's DNS also runs through Cloudflare, its **AI bot blocking may be on by default**. Check the dashboard and switch off blocking for the search crawlers above.
- **`llms.txt`:** Google says it does nothing for Google. Some other tools read it. It takes ten minutes; add it after launch as a nice-to-have, never as a priority.
- **XML sitemaps**, split by type: `pages`, `categories`, `brands`, `advice`, `products-1..n` (max 50,000 URLs each; we will have about 2,000 in tier B). Include `lastmod` only when the page really changed. Include product images (`image:image`).
- **IndexNow** on every rebuild: the build script pings Bing (and Yandex, Seznam) with changed URLs. This keeps ChatGPT and Copilot's view of the site current. Netlify has a plugin, or it is a few lines in the build script.
- Submit sitemaps in **Google Search Console** and **Bing Webmaster Tools** at launch (Bing can import from Search Console).

### 3.8 Page speed and rendering

The website plan's targets are right (LCP under 2.5s on a mid-range Android, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1). Add:

- **All important text in the HTML** as served: names, codes, specs, intros, FAQs, hours and the open-now line. The open-now status can update with JavaScript, but the hours table must be in the HTML.
- **Contact details as text**, not inside images. Phone numbers as `tel:` links, WhatsApp as `wa.me` links.

- Test with Lighthouse and PageSpeed Insights on mobile, and check a sample of pages with JavaScript turned off.

### 3.9 Internal linking

- Every product links up to its category and department (breadcrumb) and across to 8 related products (already in the plan).
- **Advice guides link to the products and categories they mention**, and categories link back to their guides ("Not sure which size? Read: Which geyser element do I need?").

### 5.4 The review engine

**Target:** 4.5+ average and **150 Google reviews within 12 months**, then steady growth. (AI recommendations cluster above about 4.3 stars and around 150 reviews. These are guides from US studies, not rules, but they set a sensible bar.)

**How:**
1. **Every enquiry reply is a review moment.** After the customer collects or receives their order, send one WhatsApp message with the direct review link (GBP "Ask for reviews" link, shortened). Template: "Thanks for coming in, [name]. If we helped, a quick Google review helps other people in Brits find us: [link]"
2. **QR code at the counter** and on the docket printout.
3. **Ask everyone, not only happy customers.** Filtering who gets asked ("review gating") breaks Google's rules. So do incentives (discounts for reviews).
4. **Invite specifics** naturally: "It helps if you mention what you came in for." Never script the words.
5. **Reply to every review within 48 hours**, by name, mentioning what they bought where it fits. Reply to negative reviews calmly with a fix and a phone number.
6. **Hellopeter** is South Africa's own review site and shows up in AI answers about SA businesses. Claim the profile and respond there too.

### 7.2 How to write pages that get cited

These follow Google's own guidance (people-first, original, expert) and what we know about how AI tools pick passages:

1. **Answer first.** The first two or three sentences under each heading answer the question directly. Details follow.
2. **Use the customer's words in headings**, including the question form ("Which geyser element do I need?").
3. **Be specific and local.** Name sizes, pressures, codes and brands. Mention local conditions only when they are true and known by the staff (for example hot summers, winter frost on exposed pipes, summer thunderstorms and lightning).
4. **Show who wrote it.** A named staff member with a line about their trade experience ("Written by [name], who has run our plumbing counter for [n] years"). Real people only; skip it if staff prefer not to be named.
5. **Tables for comparisons and sizes.** Easy for readers and for AI to lift accurately.
6. **Link to the products** used in the answer, with codes.
7. **Keep it current.** Review solar pages every quarter (models change fast) and show "Last checked [date]".
8. **Original facts are the strongest.** Things only Empire knows: what locals buy most, what fails most often in this area, typical lead times for ordered-in items. One real observation per guide.

### 7.3 The Store facts / FAQ page (`/faq`)

One page that answers every factual question a person or an AI might have. Plain questions and short answers, marked up with `FAQPage`:

- Where are you and how do I get there (from Brits centre, Harties, Mooinooi, Letlhabile, the N4)? Is there parking?
- What are your hours, including Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays?
- What do you sell? Which brands?

### 9.3 Monthly AI visibility check

Once a month, run the fixed prompt set from the workbook (about 40 prompts, for example "Where can I buy plumbing supplies in Brits?", "Best hardware store near Hartbeespoort?", "Where can I buy a Sunsynk inverter near Brits?") in **ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot and Google AI Mode**, using a clean session with location set near Brits. ## 11. Decisions for the client

### 11.1 Show prices on some products?

The brief says no prices. That protects margins and keeps customers talking to the counter, but it has SEO costs:

| With no prices | With prices (even "from R...") on selected items |
|---|---|
| No product rich results in Google | Eligible for product rich results |
| Not eligible for Google Shopping free listings or "in stock nearby" local listings | Eligible through Merchant Center (free listings) and local inventory |
| AI shopping answers favour shops that show prices | Can appear in price comparisons |
| Weak for "Sunsynk 8kW price" type searches | Competes for them |

**Recommendation:** keep the enquiry model, but consider showing **"from" prices on solar kits and the top 50 sellers**, updated monthly. If the answer stays no, the plan still works; it just leans harder on local and advice searches.

### 11.2 Solar installation

"Solar installation Brits / Hartbeespoort" is one of the largest local search groups, and many of the competitors in that space are installers. Empire needs to say clearly which of these is true:
- Empire installs (with accredited electricians): add a Solar Installation page and the matching GBP category.
- Empire refers to trusted installers: say so on the solar pages and list the partners (with their permission).
- Empire supplies only: say "We supply installers and DIY-ready kits" and target installers as trade customers.

### 11.3 Afrikaans and Setswana

**Now:** English site, with Afrikaans terms in synonyms, a line per department and the GBP description (section 4.5), and staff languages stated.
**Later:** if Search Console shows Afrikaans queries, translate the Home, Solar and FAQ pages properly (human translation, `hreflang`). Machine-translated pages would hurt more than help.

### 11.4 Other questions (add to the client list in the website plan, section 13)

1. The exact shop number and street number; the one main phone number.
2. Does the store deliver? Where, how often, and at what cost?
3. Trade accounts: yes or no?
4. Languages spoken at the counter.
5. Payment methods.
6. Which staff are willing to be named on advice pages and to appear in short videos?
7. Access to the suppliers for "where to buy" listings.
8. Any existing listings, Facebook page or old website to merge or redirect.
9. Does the store also sell building materials (cement, timber, roofing) not in the catalogue? If yes, those are big local keywords to add.

## 12. Things we are deliberately not doing

| Tactic | Why not |
|---|---|
| AI-written product descriptions for 6,000 items | The exact pattern the August 2026 spam update removed. Site-wide risk |
| Town-name landing pages | Doorway pages under Google's rules |
| Keywords in the GBP business name | Suspension risk |
| Buying reviews, review gating, discounts for reviews | Against Google's rules; reviews can be removed and the profile penalised |
| `llms.txt` as a strategy | Google confirms it does nothing for Google. Optional extra only |
| "Chunking" content into fragments for AI | Google says it is not needed; it makes pages worse for people |
| FAQ schema on every page for rich results | Google limits FAQ rich results now; use it where there is a real FAQ |
| Paid link schemes and mass directory submissions | Low value and risky. 15 to 20 real listings instead |
